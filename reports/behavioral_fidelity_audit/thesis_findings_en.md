# Behavioral fidelity under cross-map supervision and finite traces

**Research question.** Under what conditions can an action-conditioned JEPA provide a sufficiently faithful transition system for reliable model checking? We studied two distinct supervision regimes. The first transferred a predictor trained on fully enumerated training maps to 24 stress maps. The second learned separate models from sparse traces of each target map. The relevant unit of analysis is a paired setting. Rotations, repeated seeds, nested budgets and shared edges preclude interpreting pooled edge counts as independent observations.

## Empirical results

**RQ1 — Sources of failure.** In the cross-map ranking experiment, all three hash-verified checkpoints reproduced the archived discrete predictions. Top-1 selected the correct successor on 8,832 of 8,928 queries (98.92%), including 6,406 of 6,480 true moves (98.86%). Of the 96 errors, 74 mapped a move to a self-loop; the other 22 mapped a self-loop to a move. Initial bisimulation held in 40/72 seed–map cases. Diagonal membership in the greatest bisimulation held globally in 33/72. The six initial CTL verdicts agreed in 68/72 cases and the six LTL verdicts in 70/72. In 28 cases the CTL suite agreed despite failure of initial bisimulation. Thus high movement accuracy can coexist with errors in successor matching that propagate to the initial state.

The finite-trace pilot showed a different failure distribution. With positive traces alone (arm A, including accepted candidates), JEPA fitted all 189 observed query occurrences after deduplication within each setting. It correctly predicted 347/1,299 unseen successors, comprising 331/357 true self-loops and only 16/942 true moves. A fixed self-loop baseline correctly predicted 357/1,299 unseen successors; retaining observed successors raised its overall score to 546/1,488, compared with 536/1,488 for JEPA. Both passed initial bisimulation in the same four of twelve settings, all in the sealed-region family. Across eight settings the true system violated the initial invariant AG ¬danger while JEPA reported that it held. The equivalent universal LTL invariant reproduced those eight errors. These are eight distinct setting-level safety failures, counted sixteen times across the two logics.

**RQ2 — Identifiability, learning and decoding.** Exact recalculation over all total deterministic functions on the given catalogue confirmed that the legacy paired negatives contributed zero additional bits in all twelve settings. Natural candidate negatives reduced the version space in ten settings, by a mean of 0.05574 bits across all twelve. No unseen query acquired a unique successor. With natural negatives, JEPA correctly predicted four additional unseen successors in aggregate, including two additional true moves; initial bisimulation remained 4/12. Projection onto observed positive edges changed none of the 96 finite-pilot tables. Projection on the cross-map cohort was vacuous because no labelled transition of a target stress map entered training. These results localize the error to unobserved queries and expose an inductive preference. They do not isolate a causal architectural component.

On the cross-map cohort, the true successor ranked second in 92 of the 96 errors and third in the remaining four. Direct float64 recomputation of squared distances in the archive yielded the same discrete winner as float32 in all 8,928 queries. The lowest within-case margin quartile contained 95 errors among 2,232 queries. The median case-level error-detection AUROC was 0.967 over the 39 cases containing both errors and correct predictions. In the finite A cohort, only 321/942 unseen moves had their true successor in the top two. In the highest-margin quartile 175/321 queries were incorrect, including all 175 true moves in that quartile. The median movement-conditional AUROC was 0.147 over nine evaluable settings. The margin is a regime-dependent empirical diagnostic. No threshold was fitted.

Cyclically shifting the action index within each frozen prediction table reduced pooled correct predictions from 8,832 to 371 in the cross-map cohort and from 536 to 350 in finite arm A. Thus the emitted table depended on action identity. This intervention leaves open how effectively each network represented the semantics of a particular action. The explicit position subspace, residual latent predictor and nearest-neighbour decoder remain candidate explanations for self-loop preference; their separate causal contributions were not established.

**RQ3 — Observable conditions and guarantees.** Let V contain all total deterministic systems on the known finite state and action catalogues with the stipulated initial states and correct atomic labels, satisfying every reliable positive and negative trace. For any property φ evaluated with the same path semantics, if the true system belongs to nonempty V and every member gives the same verdict, that verdict is sound for the true system. The proof follows by inclusion. Conversely, two members giving opposite verdicts are indistinguishable on the supplied evidence. Any forced binary decision from that evidence must be incorrect for at least one member. A sufficient condition for agreement on all initial CTL/LTL properties is that the positive-edge closure of the initial states contains observed successors for every action at every state in the closure. Every consistent completion then has the same labelled reachable subgraph, by induction on path length; its diagonal is an initial strong bisimulation.

In a supplementary experiment, the four to eight distinct feasible candidate tables examined per setting and arm agreed on every initial formula. An offline comparison with the data-generating system, itself verified to satisfy the same constraints, nevertheless exhibited opposite verdicts for 52/144 setting–formula instances within each arm. This proves a lower bound on ambiguity in the specified class. A committee's unanimity supplied no certificate. The remaining 92 instances were not exhaustively decided. No hidden transitions were used to construct or select a candidate reconstruction.

**RQ4 — Next experiment (untested hypothesis).** Keep the predictor and decoder fixed. Replace the direct use of its point-graph verdict by a property-specific search for observation-consistent completions of both polarities. Use JEPA scores only to order the search. Certify a verdict when the opposite polarity is proven impossible and the version space is nonempty; record ambiguity and timeout separately as unknown. Compare conclusive coverage with a reachable-closure certificate under identical, fixed resource limits, and with point-model safety errors. Include a preregistered family of non-spatial finite automata with exhaustive small-instance checks. Failure to increase conclusive coverage rejects the proposed practical benefit. A single certified false-safe verdict under the stated assumptions invalidates the implementation. The method admits a domain-independent definition; cross-domain performance remains to be established.

**Evidence and scope.** The preservation and indistinguishability statements are formal. Numerical results describe actual fixed-cohort replay, re-inference, exact counting and model checking. Margin–error, coverage–performance and architectural explanations have different evidential status: the first two are descriptive associations, and the last remain causal hypotheses. None of the original point predictors abstained. No inference about population-level safety, learned labels, partially identifiable states, stochastic dynamics or alternative fairness conventions is made.
